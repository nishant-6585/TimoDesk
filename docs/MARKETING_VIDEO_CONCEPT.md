# Mikee — Marketing Video Concept

**Title:** *“Meet Mikee — the receptionist who never takes a break.”*
**Length:** 90 seconds hero cut (+ 15–30s vertical cuts for LinkedIn/Instagram from the same footage)
**Tone:** Warm, confident, a little playful. Real office, real people, natural light. No stock footage, no fake UI mockups — everything on screen is the real product.

## The through-line

One visitor's journey, start to finish, with the admin's view intercut. The
robot is the hero; the dashboard is the brain surgeons' room. Every feature is
shown as a *moment*, never as a bullet list.

## Shot list

| # | Time | Scene | What it proves |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–8s | Cold open: empty reception, morning light. Mikee's face screen "wakes" (idle → attentive as the doors open). Title card: *Meet Mikee.* | The animated face/personality — instant emotional hook |
| 2 | 8–18s | A visitor walks up. Mikee's eyes track her, then: **"Hi Priya, welcome back to xboom!"** — by name (enrolled staff cameo works too). | Face recognition + attention-gated greeting |
| 3 | 18–30s | Visitor: *"I'm here to see Vishal."* Mikee asks her name, confirms — cut to a phone buzzing: the host's notification. | Voice check-in + host notification (F1/#70) |
| 4 | 30–45s | *"Follow me — I'll take you to the meeting room."* Tracking shot of Mikee driving, visitor behind; mid-route reassurance line; arrival announcement at the door. | Voice-commanded navigation + escort |
| 5 | 45–55s | Another visitor asks: *"What does xboom actually build?"* Mikee answers fluently. Quick overlay: **"Answers from YOUR company knowledge — not the internet."** | KB-grounded voice Q&A (the moat vs. a dumb chatbot) |
| 6 | 55–70s | Cut to a laptop: the admin dashboard. Live camera feed, joystick nudge, the Knowledge Base tab — an admin types a new fact, hits Add, walks to Mikee and asks about it BY VOICE — Mikee answers it. One continuous take. | Admin app + the "teach it in 10 seconds" wow moment |
| 7 | 70–82s | Fast montage: enrollment 5-pose capture, patrol mode gliding at dusk, event log scrolling, battery card, "STOP" safety tap greying the controls. | Depth: security, safety, ops |
| 8 | 82–90s | Mikee returns to its dock, face goes sleepy. End card: **xboom · Mikee — Reception, autonomously.** CTA: *Book a demo.* | Brand close |

## The three money shots (if you only get three)

1. **The by-name greeting** (shot 2) — nothing sells "this is not a tablet on wheels" faster.
2. **Teach-then-ask** (shot 6) — type a fact in the admin app, ask the robot out loud, it answers. One take, no cuts. This is the shot for the CEO and for every enterprise buyer: *your* knowledge, instantly spoken.
3. **"Follow me"** (shot 4) — a robot that physically escorts is the demo people film on their phones.

## Production notes (practical, for this week)

- Shoot on two phones: one on a gimbal following the robot, one locked-off on a tripod for the admin-laptop scenes. Screen-record the admin app separately at 60fps and comp it in — never film a laptop screen directly.
- Capture the robot's chest screen via `adb shell screenrecord` for clean UI inserts (face animation, Voice Q&A screen).
- Record Mikee's actual ElevenLabs voice from the board/speaker feed if possible; his real voice IS the brand.
- Do every voice interaction 3× and keep the best take — STT garbles are normal; the edit hides them.
- B-roll worth grabbing while set up: eyes tracking a passer-by, the wave gesture, the greeting overlay text popping, the admin map with the robot dot moving.
- Vertical cuts: shot 2 alone (15s, "It knows your name"), shot 6 alone (20s, "Teach your robot in 10 seconds"), shot 4 alone (15s, "Follow me").

## Claims discipline (keep legal happy)

Everything above is demonstrable today on hardware. Avoid: "AI-powered security", autonomy percentages, "replaces your receptionist" (use *"staffs your reception 24/7"*). DPDP angle is a selling point for Indian enterprise: *"Visitor privacy by design — no visitor biometrics stored, auto-purged logs."*
